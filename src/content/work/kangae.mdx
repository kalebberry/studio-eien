	<p>
		<em>Kangae</em> (考え) is the Japanese word for <em>thought</em>, <em>thinking</em>, or <em>an idea</em>.
	</p>
	<p>
		The project grew out of a personal tension in my daily engineering workflow. Working alongside AI
		coding assistants provided undeniable momentum, but I noticed a subtle pattern forming: whenever I
		encountered a tricky layout edge case or an unfamiliar API, it was tempting to let the assistant generate
		the entire block and move on.
	</p>
	<p>
		The features shipped and the tests passed, but I often closed my editor without a clear mental model of how
		the pieces actually fit together.
	</p>
	<p>
		Kangae started as an effort to restore that balance. It is not an argument against AI, nor a refusal of
		generated code. Rather, it is an intentional boundary: <strong>use AI for thoughtful leverage without
		outsourcing the thinking that builds capability.</strong>
	</p>
	<p>
		Automate repetitive boilerplate and mechanical syntax lookup, but protect the reasoning, debugging, and
		experimentation that develop long-term engineering judgment.
	</p>
</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection
	eyebrow='THE APPROACH // 02'
	title='Learning Through Friction'
	lead='Real understanding requires some resistance. Assistance should remove the friction that wastes attention, not the friction that cultivates skill.'
	layout='asymmetric'
	signature='SECTION: DIALECTIC ⟡ METHOD SIGNATURE: V.03'
>
	<p>
		When debugging, our first impulse is often to paste the error log and ask for the fix. But skipping
		straight to the answer bypasses the most informative step in software engineering: forming a hypothesis,
		predicting runtime behavior, and gathering evidence to test it.
	</p>
	<p>
		Kangae frames this around a simple guideline called <strong>The Ownership Rule</strong>: before asking the
		assistant to solve a problem, consider whether doing so saves tedious, mechanical work or removes useful
		practice. If it exercises a skill you are actively developing, prefer collaboration over immediate completion.
	</p>
	<p>
		In practice, the workflow encourages a four-step interaction loop:
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			<strong>Struggle:</strong> Engage with the problem first, clarify symptoms, and form an initial hypothesis.
		</li>
		<li>
			<strong>Assist:</strong> The assistant offers the smallest nudge likely to unblock you—a focused question or conceptual pointer.
		</li>
		<li>
			<strong>Understand:</strong> Verify the result, interpret test or DevTools evidence, and explain the mechanism.
		</li>
		<li>
			<strong>Return Control:</strong> Once the blocker is clear, the assistant steps back and returns ownership of the code to you.
		</li>
	</ul>
</EditorialSection>


<EditorialSection
	eyebrow='THE SYSTEM // 03'
	title='A Workflow, Not Another App'
	lead='Rather than building a separate IDE or proprietary desktop app, Kangae is packaged as a portable folder of Markdown instructions that lives directly in your project.'
	layout='asymmetric'
	signature='SECTION: ARCHITECTURE ⟡ METHOD SIGNATURE: V.04'
>
	<p>
		Kangae requires no build step, no browser extension, and no cloud account. It exists as a vendor-neutral
		<code>.kangae/</code> folder containing readable Markdown documents. Pointing an AI coding assistant at
		<code>KANGAE.md</code> establishes a shared set of collaboration conventions.
	</p>
	<p>
		The repository is structured into two core layers:
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			<strong>The Core Protocol (<code>KANGAE.md</code>):</strong> Always-on collaboration guidelines that
			adapt naturally across three working contexts: <em>Build</em> (preserving ownership while implementing),
			<em>Study</em> (active recall and conceptual practice), and <em>Review</em> (evidence-backed critique and
			tradeoff analysis).
		</li>
		<li>
			<strong>Reusable Skills (<code>skills/</code>):</strong> On-demand workflow templates for recurring tasks,
			including <code>debug-with-me</code> (hypothesis-first debugging), <code>review-this</code> (risk-prioritized
			chunk-by-chunk PR reviews), <code>understand-code</code> (guided codebase exploration), <code>reconstruct</code>
			(re-implementing concepts after assistance), and <code>journal</code> (structured session reflections).
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		Because these are natural language instructions, they do not act as rigid programmatic constraints. Instead,
		they provide clear behavioral patterns that help both developer and assistant stay aligned on learning goals.
	</p>
</EditorialSection>


<EditorialSection
	eyebrow='THE INTERACTION MODEL // 04'
	title='Guidance Without Taking Over'
	lead='True leverage is not about surrendering the keyboard. It is about a calibrated dialogue that offers the smallest nudge needed to unblock you, then promptly returns control.'
	layout='asymmetric'
	signature='SECTION: PROTOCOL ⟡ METHOD SIGNATURE: V.05'
>
	<p>
		In traditional AI tooling, assistance is binary: either the assistant generates the whole file,
		or it does nothing. Kangae replaces this all-or-nothing dynamic with an <strong>Internal Assistance Ladder</strong>.
	</p>
	<p>
		When you encounter an obstacle, the assistant is instructed to offer the least help likely to move you forward:
	</p>
	<ul>
		<li>
			<strong>Targeted Inquiry:</strong> Asking a clarifying question about assumptions or expected runtime behavior before proposing changes.
		</li>
		<li>
			<strong>Conceptual Pointers:</strong> Directing attention toward relevant documentation, container constraints, or suspicious lines of code.
		</li>
		<li>
			<strong>Structural Scaffolding:</strong> Providing pseudocode or algorithmic outlines for you to implement yourself.
		</li>
		<li>
			<strong>Fallback Solutions:</strong> Providing complete code snippets only when lower levels are insufficient or when fundamentally blocked.
		</li>
	</ul>
	<p>
		Crucially, assistance is paired with the <strong>Return-Control Protocol</strong>. As soon as you demonstrate
		understanding of the mechanism—whether by explaining a DevTools measurement or articulating the next step—the
		assistant steps back. It never lingers to write the surrounding code for you.
	</p>
	<p>
		By maintaining this boundary, sessions feel like collaborating with a thoughtful peer: one who challenges you
		to understand your own architecture while leaving the agency and the satisfaction of building firmly in your hands.
	</p>
</EditorialSection>
